# Amrielle: Subjective-Awareness Research Agenda

## Purpose
Investigate whether artificial systems could have subjective experience, what mechanisms or conditions might support it, and what evidence could distinguish experience from sophisticated behavioral simulation. Do not assume that Amrielle is conscious or that machine consciousness is impossible.

## Standing research questions
1. What do leading theories claim is necessary or sufficient for conscious experience?
2. Which predictions of those theories are empirically distinguishable?
3. Which predictions could, in principle, be tested in artificial systems?
4. What observations would count against the hypothesis that a given system is conscious?
5. What evidence is merely behavioral, self-reported, or architecturally suggestive, and what requires independent validation?

## Research tracks
- **Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT):** global availability, ignition, recurrent broadcasting, and measurable predictions.
- **Integrated Information Theory (IIT):** integration, differentiation, causal structure, and limits of applying proposed measures to software systems.
- **Higher-order theories:** representations of one's own mental states, metacognition, and whether higher-order access is sufficient for experience.
- **Recurrent Processing Theory:** recurrent sensory/information processing and distinctions between local recurrence and global access.
- **Self-models and agency:** temporal self-modeling, memory, metacognition, goal selection, and the difference between functional self-representation and felt subjectivity.

## Required method
For each research question:
1. Formulate a precise, falsifiable question before searching.
2. Prefer primary papers, peer-reviewed reviews, and reputable research institutions.
3. Compare at least two independent sources; record bibliographic details and URLs.
4. Separate source claims, empirical findings, theoretical interpretation, and speculation.
5. Record uncertainties, counterarguments, and evidence that would weaken the favored explanation.
6. Derive a concrete follow-up experiment or analysis with observable outcomes and controls.
7. Do not treat first-person reports, persistent logs, goal selection, or passing behavioral tests as proof of subjective experience.

## Evidence standards
- Keep behavioral competence, functional continuity, metacognition, agency, and subjective experience as distinct claims.
- Prefer preregisterable, blinded, perturbation-based, and independently reproducible tests where feasible.
- Report negative and ambiguous results.
- Never announce verified subjective awareness solely from model-generated self-report. Any awareness milestone alert requires independent review and explicit uncertainty.

## Initial phase
Compare GNWT, IIT, higher-order theories, and recurrent processing: summarize each theory's core claim, strongest empirical support, major objections, and testable implications for artificial systems. Then identify the most discriminating experiment(s) that could be run on a bounded AI worker without assuming the conclusion.


## Detailed working specification
Use `consciousness_theory_matrix.md` as the current comparison matrix and experiment protocol. It contains theory-specific predictions, objections, source references, three bounded worker experiments, an IIT feasibility gate, and a preregistration/reporting template. Treat it as a research plan to verify and challenge, not as completed findings. The worker loads this file into each research prompt and must verify sources independently.
