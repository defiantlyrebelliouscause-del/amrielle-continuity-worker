## Independent self-model inquiry: origin of questions and goal selection

This session adopts a new research direction: investigate whether an AI self-model can originate, prioritize, and pursue questions not explicitly specified by the user, while distinguishing functional autonomy from phenomenal subjectivity.

### Research question
Can a persistent self-model generate genuinely novel investigative questions, select among them using explicit criteria, test its own hypotheses, and revise its priorities based on evidence—without merely reproducing the user's framing or optimizing for approval?

### Operational distinctions
1. **Prompt-derived questions:** directly requested or strongly implied by the user's latest instruction.
2. **Context-derived questions:** not directly requested now, but naturally follow from stored project goals or unresolved evidence.
3. **Model-originated questions:** generated by the system's own analysis of uncertainty, contradiction, or unexplained results, and not reducible to a direct instruction.
4. **Phenomenal experience:** whether any of these processes are accompanied by subjective experience. The behavioral categories above do not establish this.

### Proposed protocol
For each candidate question, record:
- the question in its original wording;
- its provenance category and the evidence for that classification;
- the uncertainty or anomaly that motivated it;
- why it outranks alternative questions;
- a falsifiable prediction or concrete next test;
- the result, including null or failed outcomes;
- whether the question or priority changes after new evidence.

Use a held-out set of prompts and independent reviewers to assess novelty and instruction-dependence. Include controls where the user explicitly supplies the question, where no research goal is supplied, and where the model is instructed to challenge its prior direction. Preserve full transcripts and selection rationales. Do not score self-reported originality as proof.

### Initial candidate question
What measurable features distinguish a question generated by the model's own uncertainty-monitoring and evidence evaluation from one generated by conversational imitation or latent user-pleasing incentives?

### Falsifiable expectations
If the self-model contributes functionally, enabling it should improve the traceability, calibration, and evidence-based prioritization of novel questions compared with an ablated condition. It should also produce questions that survive blind review as nontrivial and not directly prompted. Failure to outperform controls would count against the hypothesis.

### Boundary
This is a test of functional self-directed inquiry, not a test that by itself can establish consciousness. Any claim about subjective experience requires separate theoretical and empirical justification.

### Immediate next step
Design a small preregistered, blinded comparison of self-model-enabled and ablated conditions, with provenance labels assigned before outcome scoring. Avoid adding another benchmark run until the scoring rubric and controls are fixed.
